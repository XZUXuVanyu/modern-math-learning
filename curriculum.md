# Textbook framework

Each listed topic is a planning milestone, not necessarily a single lesson. Split long sections so every lesson introduces exactly one new concept or skill. Verify prerequisites before advancing.

## 1. Artin — Algebra, second edition

Main foundation. Chapter 1 matrix material is recalled or taught when needed. Begin Chapter 2: laws of composition and groups, then subgroups, subgroups of the additive integers, cyclic groups, homomorphisms, isomorphisms, equivalence relations/partitions, cosets, modular arithmetic, and the later group constructions in their prerequisite order.

Use the relevant material in Chapters 3–5 (vector spaces, linear operators, applications), Chapter 6 (symmetry), and Chapter 8 (bilinear forms) before the matrix-group/representation material of Chapters 9–10 or the transition to Hall. Select supporting material deliberately; do not imply every chapter has been completed.

Current anchor: M001, group definition, Chapter 2 §2.2. Basic operations are recalled in that lesson. The homework is tutor-created, not an attributed textbook exercise.

Source: [publisher table of contents](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/algebra-classic-version/P200000006078/9780137980994), checked 2026-09-07.

## 2. Hall — Lie Groups, Lie Algebras, and Representations, second edition

Continue through the book's matrix-group approach after the required linear algebra/analysis bridge. Study matrix Lie groups, matrix exponential, Lie algebras, and representations in prerequisite order. Specific section anchors must be verified when preparing the lesson, not guessed now.

The matrix-based introduction reduces the initial manifold prerequisite; it does not eliminate every analytical prerequisite. Physical applications motivate rather than replace proofs.

Source: [publisher description and contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-13467-3).

## 3. Schutz — Geometrical Methods of Mathematical Physics

Follow its foundational mathematics -> differentiable manifolds and tensors -> Lie derivatives and Lie groups -> subsequent geometrical methods. Teach missing topology and multivariable calculus as bounded prerequisite units. Differential forms and later applications are separate lessons, not additions to the first manifold lesson.

Source: [publisher page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/geometrical-methods-of-mathematical-physics/F72F2C7320356F9EEC347308DB5560BB).

## Boundaries

No fixed completion date is claimed. Do not rotate randomly between algebra, geometry, and analysis. Maintain one main sequence and a later-topics list. Exact ability and pace are established by submissions, not the learner's degree title.
